# Silent Witch: The Girl Who Learned to Live Beyond Her Silence

_Episodes 3–13 | Full Season Review_

**Spoiler Warning:** This article contains spoilers for the entire first season of _Secrets of the Silent Witch_. It does not discuss events beyond the anime.

---

I FINISHED IT.

And now I have a problem.

THIRTEEN EPISODES IS NOT ENOUGH.

I went into _Silent Witch_ expecting an interesting fantasy anime about an extraordinarily talented witch who struggles with social interaction. After the first two episodes, I was already interested in Monica's psychology, her relationship with mathematics, the circumstances that led to her developing chantless magic, and the political world that was gradually beginning to reveal itself.

What I did not expect was to become this attached to an entire collection of characters.

I did not expect to laugh this much.

I did not expect the chibi expressions to become one of my favourite things about the anime.

I did not expect to get emotional over a girl introducing someone as her friend.

And I certainly did not expect the finale to make two nerds reading books feel like one of the most beautiful things I had watched recently.

Somehow, _Silent Witch_ has managed to make me want an entire season where absolutely nothing happens.

Just give me Monica going to school. Give me Lana being a tsundere. Give me Cyril being an overprotective tsundere. Give me Claudia messing with Cyril. Give me Felix and Monica reading books.

Give me Nero being Nero.

MAKE IT A SLICE OF LIFE.

I genuinely think I would watch twenty-four episodes of these characters simply existing together.

And that, more than anything, is probably the biggest compliment I can give this anime.

## The Worst Spy in the Kingdom

HOLY FLUFFING HELL.

THE COMEDY.

THE EXPRESSIONS.

THE CHIBIS.

I genuinely cannot get over how funny this anime is.

There is something inherently hilarious about the premise of Monica Everett being assigned an undercover mission.

She is one of the Seven Sages. She has accomplished something that the magical world considers extraordinary. She can perform magic without chanting, understand complex magical structures, and process mathematical information at a level most people cannot even begin to comprehend.

On paper, she should be an absolutely incredible spy.

She can perform magic without announcing what she is doing. She is observant, intelligent, and capable of identifying details that other people miss.

And yet the moment her mission requires her to interact with another human being, everything falls apart.

The greatest magical prodigy in the kingdom is being defeated by basic social interaction.

It is such a wonderful execution of the worst-spy trope because Monica is not incompetent.

She is extraordinarily competent.

Just not in the particular areas that would make her life as an undercover student easy.

The contrast is what makes it so funny.

She can confront magical threats that would overwhelm almost anyone else, but introduce her to a group of students and suddenly she looks like her soul has left her body.

And the anime absolutely understands how to communicate this visually.

The chibi transformations are not just cute additions to the animation. They are almost a representation of Monica's internal experience.

A normal conversation can feel like a catastrophe to her, and the animation treats it accordingly.

Her expressions become exaggerated, her body language collapses, and the entire visual presentation changes to accommodate whatever disaster her brain believes is currently taking place.

I LOVE IT.

There is also something impressive about how the anime balances this comedy with Monica's more serious psychological development.

Her anxiety is not simply a funny personality trait. It has a history. There are genuinely painful reasons why she struggles to interact with people.

But the anime does not insist that every expression of that anxiety must become a tragic moment.

Monica is allowed to be funny.

She is allowed to be adorable.

She is allowed to be a complete disaster.

And she is also allowed to be a person who has experienced genuine trauma.

Those things coexist without necessarily invalidating one another.

That balance is one of the reasons I became so attached to her.

## Monica's Growth Is So Smooth

One of my favourite things about _Silent Witch_ is how naturally Monica develops throughout the season.

When I reached around episode six, I realised that I was considerably more invested in her personal growth and friendships than in the actual mystery surrounding the prince.

The mission was interesting, but I wanted to see Monica live.

I wanted to see her interact with Lana. I wanted to see her become friends with Casey. I wanted to see how her relationships with Felix and Cyril developed.

And what makes that development so satisfying is that it does not feel forced.

Monica does not suddenly become confident.

She does not receive one inspirational speech and decide that her social anxiety is over.

She does not wake up one morning with an entirely different personality.

Instead, she gradually accumulates experiences.

She is placed in situations that require her to interact with people. Some of those interactions go better than she expects. Some people treat her kindly. Some relationships become important to her before she has even fully understood what is happening.

But there is an important distinction here.

I do not necessarily think Monica begins this journey because she actively wants to change.

At least initially, I think a lot of her behaviour is driven by survival instinct.

She has been dragged out of the environment where she feels comfortable and placed into an academy by the Ruler of Hell himself.

Louis Miller.

That man is hilarious.

Monica is not voluntarily embarking on a journey of self-discovery. She is trying to survive a situation that she cannot easily escape.

She has a mission to complete, an identity to conceal, and an extremely intimidating colleague who expects results.

She cannot simply retreat into her usual solitude whenever something becomes difficult.

And because she has to participate in this environment, she begins experiencing things that she otherwise would never have experienced.

The friendships develop almost as a consequence of her attempts to survive.

I think that is what makes her growth feel particularly natural.

She does not have to consciously decide that she wants to become more socially capable before that development can begin.

Sometimes people change because their circumstances force them to engage with something they would otherwise avoid.

And sometimes, within those circumstances, they discover things that they genuinely want.

That is what I see happening with Monica.

Her world begins to expand.

But she remains Monica.

She is still shy. She still gets overwhelmed. She still loves mathematics. She still has moments where the idea of interacting with another person seems like the most terrifying thing imaginable.

The anime is not trying to replace her personality with a more socially acceptable one.

It is allowing the personality she already has to exist within a larger world.

## Lana Colette: THE TSUN

Obviously.

Lana is one of my favourite characters in this entire anime.

I love a good tsundere, and Lana's friendship with Monica is one of the most satisfying relationships in the season.

There is a particular moment that I keep coming back to.

Monica is scared to introduce Lana as her friend.

Lana, meanwhile, takes the entire thing in stride.

And GOD.

That is such a small moment, but it says so much.

For Lana, being introduced as Monica's friend is not some extraordinary declaration.

It is simply an acknowledgement of the relationship they already have.

For Monica, however, calling someone her friend is a much more intimidating experience.

There is an assumption involved in introducing another person as your friend.

You are assuming that they consider you their friend too.

For someone who is as uncertain about social relationships as Monica, that assumption carries a certain emotional risk.

What if she has misunderstood the relationship?

What if Lana does not see her in the same way?

What if she is being presumptuous?

The anime does not need Monica to explain all of these thoughts for the moment to work.

Her hesitation communicates enough.

And Lana's completely natural acceptance of the friendship is beautiful because it demonstrates that Monica's fear is not necessarily shared by the other person.

Lana already considers her a friend.

Monica does not have to earn the right to call her one through some extraordinary achievement.

She does not have to prove that she is useful.

She does not have to reveal that she is one of the Seven Sages.

She is just Monica.

And Lana likes her.

There is also something incredibly funny about the tsundere being the comparatively secure person in this friendship.

Lana has her own pride, her own attitude, and her own way of expressing affection.

Yet she is perfectly capable of accepting Monica as her friend while Monica is practically having an existential crisis over the terminology.

THE TSUN IS THE EMOTIONALLY FUNCTIONAL ONE HERE.

And the best thing about this relationship is that the anime eventually gives it a much more substantial payoff.

But before getting to that, there is another friendship that I need to talk about.

## Casey: I DID NOT SEE THAT COMING

THE HORSEY GIRL.

I genuinely did not imagine Casey was an assassin.

I had become comfortable with her as part of Monica's growing circle of friends.

She was another person who made the academy feel like somewhere Monica could potentially belong. Another relationship that seemed to be helping Monica experience a more ordinary life.

And then the anime revealed what she was actually planning.

What makes Casey's betrayal particularly effective is that the political plot suddenly becomes inseparable from Monica's emotional development.

Until that point, I had been treating the prince's political situation as something of a sideshow.

It was the reason Monica had been placed in the academy, and it provided a background mystery that could occasionally become relevant.

But my main interest was still Monica.

Casey's assassination attempt brings those two aspects of the anime together.

The political conflict is no longer just something happening somewhere beyond Monica's immediate world.

It has entered one of her friendships.

And that matters because of how difficult it has been for Monica to form those friendships in the first place.

For another protagonist, discovering that a friend is an assassin would already be painful.

For Monica, it is potentially even more damaging.

She is still learning to trust people.

She is still learning how to navigate relationships without constantly assuming that something will go wrong.

And now someone she has become attached to turns out to have been concealing something that places them directly in opposition to her mission.

What I particularly appreciate is that the anime does not reduce Casey to a simple villain.

Her motivations emerge from the political circumstances of the kingdom. Her hostility towards Felix is connected to Duke Clockford and the future she fears will result from his influence.

She has reasons for what she is doing.

Those reasons do not make the assassination attempt acceptable, but they give her actions a context.

And Monica's response is what really makes the arc work for me.

She has a responsibility to stop Casey.

She cannot allow her friendship to override her duty to protect the prince.

But stopping Casey does not require Monica to pretend that the friendship never mattered.

Even after Casey attempts to distance herself from Monica, Monica refuses to simply erase what their relationship meant to her.

That is such a painfully human response.

Someone can hurt you without every moment you shared with them becoming meaningless.

Someone can have deceived you without your own affection for them becoming invalid.

And Monica is not suddenly incapable of caring about Casey just because she now understands what Casey has done.

There is a certain emotional maturity in that.

Monica's gentleness does not mean she is incapable of making difficult decisions.

She can recognise that Casey has done something wrong, prevent her from carrying out the assassination, and still care about what happens to her.

I think that is an important part of Monica's character.

Her kindness is not simply a consequence of being too timid to oppose anyone.

There are moments where she makes a conscious decision to remain kind despite having every reason to feel hurt.

And the Casey arc is one of them.

## A Political World That Exists Beyond the Screen

One of the things that surprised me most about _Silent Witch_ is how rich its political world feels despite having relatively little stage presence.

There is, as always, a struggle for succession within the royal family.

There are competing interests among the nobility.

There is Duke Clockford and his considerable influence.

There are people who despise Clockford and, by association, the second prince.

But we do not spend the entire season inside the royal palace watching the other members of the royal family explain their ambitions.

We are not constantly given lengthy political expositions.

Instead, the anime drops these little titbits of information throughout the story.

And somehow, it gives us enough to begin constructing the political world ourselves.

I really like that.

There is a sense that the kingdom exists beyond the immediate story Monica is experiencing.

People have ambitions that predate her arrival at the academy. Political relationships have histories. Different characters have different reasons for supporting or opposing particular factions.

The world does not feel as though it was created solely to give Monica a mission.

And Felix's relationship with Duke Clockford is one of the most interesting examples of this.

From the perspective of someone who despises Clockford, it is easy to understand why Felix might also become an object of hatred.

The second prince appears to be associated with the duke's influence.

If someone fears what Clockford will do with political power, they might reasonably fear the consequences of Felix becoming king.

But then we see that Felix himself is trying to go against Clockford.

And suddenly the situation becomes much more complicated.

There are people who oppose Felix because of his relationship with Clockford, while Felix himself is attempting to resist the very influence they despise.

I love that we get to see both sides of that coin.

It makes the political conflict feel less like a simple division between good and evil.

People are acting on their understanding of the political situation, but that understanding is not necessarily complete.

They can identify a genuine threat and still misunderstand the intentions of someone associated with it.

And the anime communicates all of this without requiring the political plot to dominate the season.

That is impressive.

I initially thought the prince's mystery was primarily an excuse to place Monica in the academy.

And in terms of the story I was personally most invested in, that remained largely true.

But I do not think that makes the political world shallow.

If anything, the fact that it feels this substantial while remaining mostly in the background demonstrates how effectively the anime has used its limited time.

The politics provide context for the personal stories.

And the personal stories allow us to experience the consequences of the politics.

Neither needs to completely consume the other.

## Isabelle and Felix: A Cliché Executed Perfectly

There is one scene I absolutely have to mention.

The confrontation where Isabelle and Felix absolutely dismantle the girl who mistreated Monica.

Absolutely.

Did I love every second of it?

ABSOLUTELY.

Sometimes a trope does not need to be reinvented.

Sometimes it just needs to be executed well.

We have spent so much time watching Monica struggle with social situations that seeing people use their confidence and social authority to defend her is incredibly satisfying.

Monica is someone who can confront powerful magical threats, yet she struggles to defend herself in ordinary social conflicts.

That discrepancy makes her especially vulnerable in the academy.

And then Isabelle and Felix enter the situation.

The political and social heavyweights have arrived.

What makes Isabelle particularly hilarious is the role she was originally supposed to play in Monica's life.

She is meant to be the villainess.

She is supposed to pretend to mistreat Monica as part of the arrangement that allows Monica to maintain her cover.

And yet she is one of the most enthusiastic supporters Monica could possibly have.

The fake villainess is significantly nicer than some of the actual people Monica encounters.

I LOVE HER.

And the scene works because we have already developed an emotional investment in Monica.

We understand why it is difficult for her to stand up for herself.

We understand why the treatment she receives is upsetting.

So when someone finally delivers consequences, the satisfaction is earned.

The anime does not need to be ashamed of giving us that kind of emotional payoff.

I am perfectly happy to enjoy a cliché when the characters have made me care enough for it to work.

## Monica's Trauma Has Levels

This is probably the most important part of Monica's character development for me.

I really like that her trauma has levels.

In the beginning, I had a particular understanding of her history.

We see the little girl who loves mathematics. We learn about what happened to her father. We see the person she eventually becomes: someone who has largely isolated herself from the world and struggles to communicate with other people.

It initially seemed as though the tragedy involving her father had pushed her further towards mathematics, and that the resulting trauma had also contributed to her eventual isolation.

But the anime gradually reveals that the situation is more complicated.

There is another important part of Monica's history.

Bernie Jones.

The megane-kun.

And his arc changes how I understand a significant portion of Monica's development.

Because the Monica we see in those memories is not simply the completely isolated person we meet at the beginning of the anime.

She has already experienced pain.

She already struggles with certain aspects of social interaction.

But she can still form a meaningful connection with another person.

She can still admire someone.

She can still express excitement.

She can still experience friendship.

And she is so incredibly sweet with Bernie.

GOD, MONICA. YOU ARE GOING TO MAKE ME CRY.

Bernie initially helps her when she is struggling.

He is someone who recognises her abilities and gives her support.

He introduces her to shortened chanting, which becomes an important part of her magical development.

And Monica is genuinely grateful to him.

What makes their relationship so painful is that it is not presented as something that was always terrible.

There was a real friendship there.

There was a reason Monica valued him.

There was a reason his eventual rejection could hurt her so deeply.

As Monica's abilities develop, Bernie's relationship with her begins to change.

The person who helped her improve finds himself confronted with the reality that she has surpassed him.

And his insecurity becomes entangled with their friendship.

There is a particularly painful irony in the fact that the person who helped Monica move towards one of her greatest achievements eventually resents her for that achievement.

Something that should have been a source of shared happiness becomes a source of division.

And I think this is an important distinction in understanding Monica's isolation.

The tragedy involving her father and her experiences with Bernie are not simply two interchangeable examples of trauma.

They affect different aspects of her emotional life.

Her father is deeply connected to her love of mathematics, her grief, and her relationship with something that has given her life meaning.

Bernie is connected to friendship, trust, vulnerability, and the experience of having someone she valued turn against her.

The anime gradually reveals how these different experiences contribute to the person Monica has become.

And because of that, her development does not feel like a simple journey towards overcoming one particular fear.

There are different things she has to confront.

She can make progress in one area of her life while remaining deeply affected by something else.

She can become more comfortable around Lana and still be terrified of encountering Bernie.

That does not mean her earlier growth was meaningless.

It means she has encountered a different layer of her past.

And I love that the anime understands this.

## Felix and Monica: The Two Crybabies

One of my favourite observations about Felix is how much he mirrors Monica.

At first, the two of them appear to be almost complete opposites.

Monica is visibly timid.

She is socially anxious, struggles to express herself, and frequently becomes overwhelmed by situations that Felix seems perfectly capable of navigating.

Felix is charming.

He is gentle, composed, and capable of carrying himself in a way that suits his position as a prince.

But through the chess captain's memories and Felix's own recollections, we begin to see a different version of him.

A timid boy.

A sensitive boy.

A crybaby.

And suddenly the resemblance to Monica becomes much more apparent.

I love this.

Because it gives us a completely different way of understanding Felix's interest in her.

The girl in front of him possesses qualities that resemble a version of himself.

And the fact that their present-day personalities seem so different makes that resemblance even more interesting.

They have developed different ways of navigating the world.

Monica's vulnerability remains visible.

Felix has become much more capable of managing the way other people perceive him.

But I do not necessarily think Felix's gentleness is fake.

That is an important distinction for me.

When I look at him, his expression seems genuinely gentle.

And there are moments, such as his intervention to help the chess captain, where I think that gentleness feels particularly sincere.

I do not see him as someone who is secretly an entirely different person underneath a carefully manufactured smile.

Instead, I think he might be using something genuine about himself to construct the face he presents to the world.

His gentleness can be real while also functioning as part of his public image.

Those things are not mutually exclusive.

A person can genuinely care about someone while understanding the social or political implications of helping them.

A person can possess a naturally gentle temperament while deliberately presenting themselves in a composed and reassuring manner.

And Felix's political circumstances give him plenty of reasons to be careful about what he reveals.

He is involved in a succession struggle.

He is associated with a powerful duke whose influence he is attempting to resist.

He cannot necessarily afford to express every vulnerability or every thought that he has.

But that does not mean the person underneath his public presentation has disappeared.

I think the sensitive boy we see in those memories is still an important part of him.

And that is what makes the parallel with Monica so compelling.

Monica and Felix appear to have begun with similar temperaments.

Their circumstances have pushed them towards different ways of surviving.

Monica developed an extraordinary ability that allowed her to function without speaking.

Felix developed the social composure necessary to navigate an environment where speaking does not necessarily mean revealing everything.

One struggles to express herself.

The other has become skilled at controlling what he expresses.

And beneath those differences, there are similarities that make their interactions feel much more interesting than a conventional prince-and-shy-girl dynamic.

## Natural Human Closure

This is one of the things I appreciate most about the structure of _Silent Witch_.

Monica's mini-arcs reach what feels like natural human closure.

They do not necessarily end with every problem being solved.

They do not require Monica to become a completely different person.

They simply reach a point where something meaningful has changed.

Her relationship with Lana begins with uncertainty and develops into a friendship that Monica can proudly rely on.

Her relationship with Casey forces her to confront the possibility that someone she cares about can do something terribly wrong, without requiring her to erase her own affection for that person.

Her encounter with Bernie allows her to confront a painful part of her history while recognising the support she now has in her present.

And the finale allows her to experience someone else's genuine appreciation for something connected to her father.

These are different kinds of emotional resolutions.

And I like that the anime does not try to make them all mean the same thing.

Monica does not need to respond to every painful experience through the same process.

Her trauma has different layers, and her growth reflects that.

There is no single moment where the anime declares that she has finally become socially confident and can now leave her past behind.

She remains shy.

She remains awkward.

She remains deeply affected by certain memories.

But her world is different.

And because her world is different, her relationship with those memories can begin to change.

I think that is what makes the development feel so smooth.

We are not watching Monica climb a clearly defined ladder of psychological achievements.

We are watching her live.

She encounters people.

She experiences things.

She sometimes struggles.

She sometimes finds unexpected happiness.

And gradually, those experiences become part of who she is.

The anime allows growth to happen through the accumulation of ordinary human moments.

That is something I really appreciate.

## The Animation: From Chibi Chaos to Beautiful Magic

I cannot finish this article without giving the visual presentation its own discussion.

Because HOLY FLUFFING HELL.

The range of expression in this anime is wonderful.

There are scenes where Monica is reduced to the most adorable little chibi disaster imaginable.

There are scenes where the characters' expressions alone make an interaction hilarious.

And then there are moments of genuinely beautiful magical spectacle.

The ice-versus-fire confrontation remains one of my favourites.

But what I appreciate is not simply that the anime can make magic look beautiful.

It is that the visual presentation understands the different emotional purposes of its scenes.

When Monica is overwhelmed by a conversation, the exaggerated animation communicates her internal chaos.

When she is performing magic, the presentation can become much more precise and composed.

And when the story reaches a quieter emotional moment, it does not necessarily need to rely on exaggerated expressions or enormous magical effects.

It can allow a small interaction to carry the scene.

That flexibility is important.

Monica is a character whose internal experience can vary enormously depending on the situation.

An ordinary conversation can feel like an impossible challenge.

An extraordinarily complex magical problem can be something she understands with remarkable clarity.

The animation allows us to experience that contrast.

And the comedy benefits enormously from it.

I genuinely think the chibi expressions are one of the highlights of the entire season.

They have so much personality.

They make Monica's anxiety visually entertaining without requiring the anime to treat every moment of fear as a serious psychological event.

And the fact that the same character can be presented as an adorable little disaster in one scene and an extraordinarily capable witch in another never stops being satisfying.

I love her.

I love the expressions.

I love the magical spectacle.

And I love how effectively the anime brings those things together.

## PLEASE MAKE IT A SLICE OF LIFE

I started this season interested in the magical world and Monica's psychological development.

I ended it wanting an entire season of these characters simply spending time together.

Give me Monica and Lana going shopping.

Give me Cyril insisting that he is only looking after Monica because it is his responsibility.

Give me Claudia destroying Cyril's composure with a single sentence.

Give me Isabelle being the most enthusiastic fake villainess in existence.

Give me Felix and Monica spending an afternoon reading books.

Give me Nero causing problems.

Give me Louis appearing occasionally to remind Monica that the Ruler of Hell has not forgotten about her.

I genuinely do not need a major assassination plot every few episodes.

And I think that says something about the strength of the characters.

The political world is interesting.

The magic system is interesting.

The mystery surrounding Felix is interesting.

But by the end of the season, my primary attachment is to the people who inhabit that world.

I want to see more of them.

And there is a particularly beautiful reason why slice of life would work so well for Monica.

For most characters, ordinary experiences are ordinary.

For Monica, they are an adventure.

Making a friend is a significant experience.

Going to a festival is a significant experience.

Sharing a book with someone is a significant experience.

Introducing someone as her friend can carry an enormous amount of emotional weight.

The things that might be considered filler in another story are some of the most important parts of Monica's life.

Because they are experiences she has spent so much time missing out on.

And I think that is why the anime can make something as simple as two people reading books feel like a satisfying conclusion.

We understand what those ordinary experiences mean to her.

We understand how difficult it has been for her to reach a point where she can have them.

And we want her to have more.

## Final Thoughts

When I wrote my first impressions of _Silent Witch_, I described Monica as a girl who found her voice in silence.

She had developed an extraordinary magical ability because of something that made ordinary life difficult for her.

Her inability to speak comfortably had not prevented her from pursuing the thing she loved.

Instead, she had found another way to pursue it.

And that remains one of the most beautiful aspects of her character.

But after thirteen episodes, I think there is something more to her story.

Monica had already found a way to survive before the anime began.

She had mathematics.

She had magic.

She had her solitude.

She had constructed a life in which she could function despite the things she struggled with.

The academy forces her into an environment that threatens that familiar way of living.

Initially, she is simply trying to survive it.

But gradually, she begins discovering things that her previous life could not give her.

Friendship.

Support.

Shared interests.

The opportunity to confront parts of her past with someone beside her.

The experience of seeing something deeply personal to her appreciated by another person.

And none of this requires Monica to abandon the person she already is.

That is what I love most about her development.

She does not have to stop being shy.

She does not have to stop loving mathematics.

She does not have to erase her trauma.

She does not have to become someone else.

She simply gets to experience more of the world.

And the people she meets become part of that experience.

Lana's friendship matters.

Casey's friendship matters, even after what happened.

Cyril's protectiveness matters.

Felix's sincerity matters.

Even the ridiculous circumstances created by Louis matter because they brought Monica into an environment where these relationships could develop.

The story that began with a witch being assigned to protect a prince has become something much more personal to me.

It is a story about a girl who has experienced pain, found ways to survive it, and is gradually discovering that her life can contain more than the things she needed in order to survive.

And it manages to tell that story while being absolutely hilarious.

While giving us gorgeous magic.

While constructing a surprisingly rich political world.

While making me fall in love with a tsundere best friend, an overprotective tsundere vice president, a derpy chaos merchant, a fake villainess, a gentle prince, and a cat with absolutely no intention of allowing his master to live peacefully.

I came for the Silent Witch.

I stayed for Monica.

And now I just want to see her live.
